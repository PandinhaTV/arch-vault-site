# Super Mario 64

**Nintendo · 1996 · Nintendo 64 · ~12 hours**
Director and producer: Shigeru Miyamoto.

Reference sheet: `Attachments/Documents/Reference Sheets/Ficha Técnica Super Mario 64-1.pdf`

## What the sheet records

- **Key features:** open, expansive map; complex acrobatic moves; 120 collectible stars; 15 sandbox levels; secret stars
- **Mechanics:** 3D movement, accumulating jumps, air dives, attacks, scenery interaction, controllable camera, special hats
- **Dynamics:** star collection and progression; combining jumps and dives; using attacks to shed speed
- **Aesthetic:** colourful visuals, fluid animation, a vast mysterious castle as the main hub
- **Audience:** exploration fans, children and teenagers, people interested in 3D innovation

## What ARCH takes from it

**Movement as the reward.** The moveset is deep, every move is available from the start, and combining them is the skill. [[Cache]] works the same way — nothing in ARCH gates an ability behind progress.

**A hub that is a place, not a menu.** The castle is worth exploring for its own sake. ARCH's [[Archipelago]] arrived at the same conclusion the hard way, through [[Playtest 01]].

**Collection as the gate.** 120 stars there, PDFs and ZIPs here. Both let the player choose their order and both hold the finale behind a count.

## The difference worth noting

Mario 64 is hub-and-spoke — the castle is a lobby, and paintings are doors into separate worlds. ARCH is a **seamless archipelago** where the levels are visible from the hub and from each other. That is closer to [[The Big Catch - Tacklebox]] than to Mario 64.

Worth being clear about this in §1.6, because "Mario 64" as a reference could mean the hub, the moveset, or the collection loop, and the team means the second and third.
