## 3 · After — close it, then write

### Claims worth keeping

- **This is not a sequel you read, it is a shelf you consult.** Twelve chapters on twelve separate specialisms — diversity and research, romance, universe-building, board games, novel↔game and film↔game adaptation, implementation, cinematics, quest design, planning, story editing, freelancing. Only read the one you currently need
- **Narrative work cannot be estimated like prose.** A line of dialogue costs the line, plus recording, plus animation, plus the scene, plus localisation. Ch. 11 is the chapter to read before agreeing to a deadline
- **If the world touches a real culture, religion or condition, ask someone who lives it — before building.** Ch. 1 treats consultants as part of the schedule, not a review step
- **A universe should survive sequels without retconning**, which means deciding early which rules are permanent and which are local to one story

### Quotes

None — nothing here needed its exact wording.

### Disagreements

- **Most of it is aimed at a working professional**, and three chapters are about careers rather than craft — freelancing, story editing, working with licences. Not relevant to a student project, and worth knowing that so I stop feeling like I should read it all
- Its adaptation chapters assume you have source material. We do not, so two of twelve chapters are dead weight for now
- Honest caveat: I skimmed this one. The claims above are from chapter openings and contents, not a full read — so treat them as signposts rather than conclusions

### Apply to

- [ ] Read ch. 11 *Planning Your Work* properly before committing to narrative scope for the next milestone
- [ ] Nothing else yet. This is a book to come back to, not to act on now
