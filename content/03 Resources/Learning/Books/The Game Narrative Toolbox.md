## 3 · After — close it, then write

### Claims worth keeping

- **The chapter order is the method.** Concept → worldbuilding → characters → story → implementation → dialogue → cutscenes → troubleshooting. Doing the exercises in order leaves you holding a full set of documentation, which is the book's stated promise
- **Wireframe the story linearly first, branch on a second pass.** A branching diagram drawn first hides the absence of a spine behind complexity
- **Six character fields carry a whole story:** governing trait, conflicting trait, breaking point, strongest dream, strongest fear, dark secret. The rest of its template is scaffolding
- **Narrative gets cut first because it is cheapest to change.** So write cheap-to-change things late and lock expensive things early, or every schedule problem arrives disguised as a story problem
- **Macro worldbuilding before micro**, and only as much macro as the story actually touches

### Quotes

> Throughout the book, you're encouraged to work on your own story and move it through a simplified development process. If you complete all of the exercises, you will have a full set of game documentation and dialogue samples.
> — introduction

> It is far easier to rewrite the words of a dialogue than to painstakingly reanimate the scene that goes with it. A picture may or may not be worth a thousand words, but it certainly takes a thousand times longer to make.
> — ch. 9

### Disagreements

- **It assumes a narrative designer exists as a role**, with a team around them and executives to pitch to. Half of ch. 2 and most of ch. 6 are about negotiating with people a five-person student project does not have
- **Worldbuilding before story is the wrong order for us.** The world already existed before anyone wrote a story — that is the actual situation, and the book has no chapter for arriving late to your own world
- Ch. 9 says to design a story testing plan, and then does not say what the test looks like. The useful half is *decide the pass condition before you run it*; the rest is about QA departments

### Apply to

- [ ] Fill the six character fields for the protagonist before writing any plot
- [ ] Write the one-liner twice — once as a description, once as a comparison — and try both on someone outside the course
