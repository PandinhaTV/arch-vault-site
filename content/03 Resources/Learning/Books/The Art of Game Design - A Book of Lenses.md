# The Art of Game Design — A Book of Lenses

**Jesse Schell · Morgan Kaufmann · 518 pages · 100 lenses**

PDF at `Attachments/Documents/Books/`, kept out of git — see the README there.

## What it is

Not a methodology. A hundred short chapters, each ending with a **lens**: a small set of questions to hold up against your game. You are not meant to read it front to back and apply all of it. You pick the lenses that hit what you are currently stuck on.

That makes it unusually well matched to how this vault already works. Every design note here has an `## Open questions` section. Schell's lenses are open questions someone else already wrote and tested.

All 100 lenses are set up as worksheets with Schell's own questions and space to answer: [[Lens Reviews Index]].

## Lenses worth using now

Chosen against problems that are actually open in this vault today, not the ones that sound most interesting.

**Lens 18 — Flow.** [[The Art of Game Design A Book of Lenses.pdf#page=151|p122]] [[Aquatic]] is themed on flow and [[Playtest 01]] found 7 of 9 testers named movement as the main difficulty while also naming the hook and dash as favourites. Schell's flow conditions — clear goals, no distractions, direct feedback, continuously matched challenge — are a checklist for exactly that gap.

**Lens 16 — The Player.** [[The Art of Game Design A Book of Lenses.pdf#page=135|p106]] Playtest 01's clearest excluded signal was the tester who had never played a 3D platformer: replay 1, explore 1. The stated audience is Explorers and Achievers. This lens asks what they like that you are not giving them.

**Lens 3 — Fun.** [[The Art of Game Design A Book of Lenses.pdf#page=56|p27]] Fun scored 4.2 and everything else 3.7–4.0. Schell's question is which parts are not fun and why, which is the question the numbers are pointing at without answering.

**Lens 19 — Needs.** [[The Art of Game Design A Book of Lenses.pdf#page=156|p127]] Directly relevant to [[Time Medals]] and [[Skins]]. Which need does a medal serve, and does it serve one the game is not already serving?

**Lens 32 — Meaningful Choices.** [[The Art of Game Design A Book of Lenses.pdf#page=210|p181]] The two critical paths in [[Aquatic]] are the sharpest idea in the game, but only if players know a choice exists. See [[Decision - Buttons can be skipped to preserve flow]].

**Lens 70 — Story.** [[The Art of Game Design A Book of Lenses.pdf#page=309|p280]] *"Nobody mentioned the story"* — not one of nine responses. → [[Narrative Rework]]

**Lens 15 — The Toy.** [[The Art of Game Design A Book of Lenses.pdf#page=120|p90]] Is it fun before it has a goal? The [[Archipelago]] is the answer here, and [[Decision - The world became the main attraction]] was that discovery made accidentally. This lens names it.

**Lens 57 — Feedback.** [[The Art of Game Design A Book of Lenses.pdf#page=259|p230]] [[Wall Run & Wall Jump]] is the biggest fixable problem in the game, and the cause is invisible rules — a 0.5s timer and a 20° angle nobody can see. This lens is the whole fix.

**Lens 10 — Resonance.** [[The Art of Game Design A Book of Lenses.pdf#page=86|p56]] For [[Art Direction - Frutiger Aero]]. Why does this aesthetic and this premise belong together? The vault says it well already; this is the lens that tests it.

## For the semester

**Análise e Crítica de Jogos** is the unit this book was written for. → [[Semester Units 2026-27]]

A concrete use: run one lens per week against ARCH and write the answer into the note it touches. Ten weeks, ten lenses, ten real improvements — and it produces coursework and game progress from the same work.

## How to note from it

One note per lens you actually use, not per chapter. A lens you have not applied to something is a lens you have not read.
