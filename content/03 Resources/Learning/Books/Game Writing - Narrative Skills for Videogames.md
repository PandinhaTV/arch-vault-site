## 3 · After — close it, then write

### Claims worth keeping

- **Narrative is how the story reaches the player; story is what happens.** In a game you hand the narrative to the player — where they look, what order, who they talk to — and keep the story. This is why branching the narrative is nearly free and branching the story is ruinous
- **The spine is the mandatory events.** Side content is by definition not on it. Which makes the spine the only part of the story every player is guaranteed to see — so anything they must understand has to sit there, or you are hoping
- **Freedom versus clarity has no solution, only a position.** Clearer instructions make the player feel constrained; more freedom to ignore them makes the path vanish. Each project needs a stated policy, or the trade gets re-decided in every meeting
- **Traits before characteristics.** Traits are who someone is; gender, race, career and looks are characteristics. Define the characteristic first and you get a stereotype every time
- **The immediate-level story can beat the written one.** The player's own account of what they did is more powerful than any plot — so cheap consequences for unscripted actions buy more investment than branches do

### Quotes

> Narrative is here defined as the methods by which the story materials are communicated to the audience. Story, meanwhile, can be taken to refer to all story details and primarily to a set of events as driven by characters within a fictional space.
> — Richard Boon, ch. 3

> "Hold on. What if the player doesn't go in the cabin?" … "They have to go in to the cabin." … "Well, what if they don't?" … "Why would they do that?" "Because they can."
> — Mary DeMarle, ch. 4, opening

### Disagreements

- **It is a collection, and it shows.** Chapters contradict each other on how much authorial control is achievable, and nobody reconciles them. Useful as five strong opinions, not as one method — the opposite of [[The Game Narrative Toolbox]]
- **Almost every example is a dialogue-heavy game** — RPGs, adventure games, shooters with scripted sequences. A platformer where nobody speaks has to translate everything, and the book never considers that case
- The DeMarle toilet-flushing example is great and also AAA-priced. Three bespoke reactions to an optional act is more content than a whole area might get

### Apply to

- [ ] Write down the spine as a list of mandatory events, and check whether anything the player must understand is sitting off it
- [ ] Pick a position on freedom versus clarity in writing, so it stops being re-argued
