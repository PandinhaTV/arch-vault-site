# Playtest 01 — Team Summary

*Written to be shared. The full breakdown, with the numbers and the filtering, is in [[Playtest 01]].*

## Playtest 01 — what we learned

**Why we ran it.** This was the first time anyone outside the team played ARCH. We were not testing one feature. We wanted to know whether the movement kit was fun in someone else's hands, whether the objectives read clearly, and whether the thing held together at all for a person we had not talked through it first.

Most of the people who played know 3D platformers well and named the games you would expect — Mario, Sonic, Banjo, Mirror's Edge, Neon White. A few had never heard of Frutiger Aero, which matters: the aesthetic cannot carry meaning on its own for everyone. It has to work as *this looks nice* first.

A few responses were set aside as inconsistent or low-effort before drawing conclusions. Everything below survives that.

**Movement is both the draw and the barrier.** This is the finding. Asked what they found difficult, most people said the same thing: using the movement. Asked what they liked most, they named the hook and the dash — parts of the same system. That is not a contradiction to fix by simplifying. It means the moveset is the game and it needs tuning, not trimming.

**Walls were the clearest problem.** Several people raised wall jump and wall run unprompted — sticking to walls, not responding, needing to be more responsive. Worth knowing why: every rule governing a wall is invisible. There is a short time window, there is a minimum steepness, and there is a rule stopping you jumping back into the wall you came from. A player who fails an invisible rule does not think *I did that wrong*. They think the game ignored them.

A real bug came out of the same answers: the double jump does not reset when you touch a wall.

**Other things reported**

- The character feels too fast and slippery to control
- Falling constantly
- The camera getting stuck in places
- Confusion at the first floor button — nothing in the game teaches ground pound before it is required
- One person could not reach the top of a climb and had no way to know why

**What they enjoyed.** The hook, most of all. Also the dash, the boost rings, the animations, the variety of things you can do, and the skins — one person named skins as the most fun part without being asked about them. One said learning the controls was itself the enjoyable bit, which is a good sign for a game about movement.

**Nobody mentioned the story.** Not one person referred to the premise, to Nexus, or to what deleting the files is supposed to mean. For a game whose whole pitch is a worker taking his time back from his employer, that is worth sitting with.

**What this session changed.** People wanted to be in the world more than they wanted to attempt the challenges, and we changed direction because of it. That decision was the right one — the next playtest confirmed it decisively. → [[Playtest 02 - Team Summary]]

**What is still open from this session**

1. Walls. Either make them more forgiving, or show the player when a wall is available. Still the biggest fixable problem we have.
2. The double jump reset bug.
3. Teach the first ground pound, or move it later in the level.
4. The story reaching the player at all.
