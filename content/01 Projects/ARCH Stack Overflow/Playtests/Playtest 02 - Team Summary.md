# Playtest 02 — Team Summary

*Written to be shared. The full breakdown, with the numbers, is in [[Playtest 02]].*

---

## Playtest 02 — what we learned

**Why we ran it.** After the first playtest we changed direction and made the world the main attraction rather than a corridor between levels. This session existed to answer one question: was that the right call, or did we trade away the challenge that people liked?

**The answer is yes, clearly.** Every single person who played said they wanted to explore the environment. Not most of them — all of them. It was the strongest response we have ever had to any question, in either playtest. Fun went up too. Whatever else we discuss, the decision to build outward was correct and we should stop treating it as an open question.

**The moveset is the thing people talk about.** Almost everyone named the cursor head and the hoverboard as their favourite part of the game, ahead of every other mechanic. Two people compared it to games they love. Several also said the most fun they had was finding ways to break the game and reach places they were not supposed to — which is worth taking as a compliment about how expressive the movement is, and worth being careful about before we patch it all away.

**The problem is navigation, not content.** People wanted to be out in the world and could not tell where they were in it. Several got lost. One tester put it better than we have:

> *"é tudo vidro, eu vejo tudo, aka idk where im going em relação ao resto — coloquem coisas que o player consiga usar como landmarks"*

That is the clearest thing anyone told us. The glass and transparency that define our look are also making everything read the same, so nothing stands out as a destination. The plan has always assumed players navigate by seeing structures in the distance and heading toward them. Right now there is nothing distinct enough to head toward.

**Movement feels slippery.** Several people, independently, said the character slides too much and does not respond the way they expect. One suggested the fix directly: more friction, especially while surfing. This came up in the first playtest too, from one person, and we treated it as one person's opinion. It is not.

**Smaller things people asked for**

- The control list available in the pause menu
- Clearer visual feedback about where they are heading
- A grab bug that forced a restart

**What I think we take from this**

1. Landmarks are the highest-value thing we can build. It is the only problem a tester handed us with the solution attached.
2. Add friction to the hoverboard.
3. The world question is settled. We can stop debating it and spend that energy on making the world readable.

One more thing worth saying: this data is from June and we have been working from a summary of it rather than the responses themselves. The summary said the session went well, which is true, but it left out the navigation problem entirely. Worth reading the actual answers before the next one.
