# Art Direction — Frutiger Aero

## The aesthetic

![[concept-posters.png]]

Frutiger Aero is the visual language of early-2000s operating systems. The name is recent; the style comes from the icons used by OS companies such as Windows XP. Glossy, bright, humanist sans-serif type, water, greenery, bubbles, optimistic technology.

The project started from wanting to explore this aesthetic. It is not easy to capture, so João Soares ran a presentation for the team so everyone understood what the project was before production began.

## Where it sits in the family

![[frutiger-family-map.png]]

*Source: Aesthetics Wiki / @BlumeCity. Added to the vault 2026-08-03.*

Frutiger Aero is one branch of the **Frutiger Family**, roughly 2004–2013 with edges at 2001 and 2017. The family's shared traits are the checklist the art direction is working from: high definition, nature, humanism, futurism, skeuomorphism, aero glass, gradients, glossy textures, auroras, bokeh, bubbles, water, grass, abstract flourishes, abstract tech, Web 2.0, and 7th-generation gaming.

Two things this map settles.

**The sub-aesthetic names are real.** *Helvetica Aqua Aero* and *Bright Tertiaries* are named members of the taxonomy sitting under Frutiger Aero, not labels the team invented. So they carry meaning to anyone who knows the family, and they can be researched rather than guessed at.

**Corporate is a sibling, not a generic term.** §4.4 says the final level takes "some inspiration from Corporate aesthetics" — and Corporate is its own bubble in this family, next to Frutiger Aero rather than inside it. That gives the final tower a precise target instead of a vague one, and it explains why moving from the world into the tower feels like a change of dialect rather than a change of language. They are relatives.

Also worth knowing the neighbours the project is *not* using: Cybercore (Y2K futurism), Frutiger Metro, Vectorflourish, Superflat Pop, McBling. A style guide could usefully say which of these are out of bounds.

⚠ The map appears to spell it **Bright Tertiaries**; the GDD writes **Bright Tertiaires**. Worth checking against the Aesthetics Wiki and settling on one, since it is the name of a whole level's look. → [[Open Issues in the GDD]]

## Three sub-aesthetics

| Space | Sub-aesthetic | Palette |
|---|---|---|
| [[Archipelago]] | Frutiger Aero | Green, blue, glass, sky |
| [[Aquatic]] | Helvetica Aqua Aero | Vibrant tropical over dominant blue |
| [[Office]] | Bright Tertiaires | Tertiary orange, green, blue, plus corporate white |

Each space has its own version of one aesthetic. The world stays coherent while each level still reads as a distinct place. The palette also carries the story: the closer the player gets to Nexus, the more corporate the colours become.

## Why the aesthetic fits the story

Frutiger Aero was designed to make software feel clean, natural and friendly. The game uses it as the surface of a surveillance system.

This gives the art direction a constraint. The world should stay genuinely attractive. If it starts looking sinister, the contrast is lost and the unease has to come from the palette rather than from what is happening.

## Cross-level sightlines

Assets from other areas are visible from inside levels. The Frutiger Aero wind turbines and the Bright Tertiaires Nexus building can both be seen from inside the aquatic level. This is what makes the archipelago feel like one place.
